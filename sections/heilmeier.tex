\section{The Heilmeier Questions}

\begingroup
\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\textbf{Q\arabic{enumi}.}}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{What are you trying to do? Articulate your objectives using
    absolutely no jargon.}\par
    I am attempting to build a control system, test stand, and flight-ready hardware suited to a higher thrust class small-satellite thruster with lower power and higher efficiency than other thruster types.
  \item \textbf{How is it done today, and what are the limits of current
    practice?}\par
    Propulsion research generally doesn't take into account the technical details behind implementation\cite{tummalaOverviewCubeSatellitePropulsion2017}; even with the best resistojets, many cubesats would be at the limit of their power generation capabilities \cite{clarkHugePowerDemandItsyBitsy2010}.
  \item \textbf{What is new in your approach, and why do you think it will be
    successful?}\par
     A thermal battery can be around 30\% more energy dense than Li-ion batteries, which makes this system better suited for a cubesat. Companies like SteamJet have proved out the path with similar devices, and initial phases of hardware have been developed.
  \item \textbf{Who cares? If you are successful, what difference will it
    make?}\par
    Cubesats will gain a propulsion system which is extremely well-suited for their size, power budget, safety requirements, and cost, enabling longer effective mission time, station keeping, collision avoidance, and orbit raising capabilities to satellites otherwise without those features.
  \item \textbf{What are the risks?}\par
    PCB layout and schematic mistakes may cause unrecoverable delays in the project; software to control the flight board may take longer than expected to entirely run; not all design features and sensing capabilities necessary for full operation may be predicted before going forward with designs.
  \item \textbf{How much will it cost?} (optional)\par
    All funding is coming from the Amateur Satellite Corporation (AMSAT) and will be under \$1000.
  \item \textbf{How long will it take?}\par
     The timeline for this project is approximately one year.
  \item \textbf{What are the midterm and final ``exams'' to check for
    success?}\par
    By month 3, the propulsion system control hardware should be built and functional; By month 6, the propulsion control hardware should be controlling burns and transmitting data via USB; by month six, a solution to the bus power problem should be conceived and designed in; a backup orbit/attitude determination algorithm should be implemented entirely from the control card by month 10; and flight-ready hardware should be available for manufacture prior to mission readiness (at the end of the project).
\end{enumerate}
\endgroup
