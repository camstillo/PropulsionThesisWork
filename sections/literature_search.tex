\subsection{Search Questions and Queries}
\textbf{Search Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How have people handled doing the power bus optimization which takes into account probabilistic models for radio usage, ADCS and control power, and propulsion control?
    \item Can I estimate how much power I'm going to need for the thermal battery to heat the water fully?
    \item How can the satellite handle the optimal pointing scheme (maximize sun radiation) while rotating the satellite to the position for firing?
    \item How can a backup orbit/attitude determination scheme be chosen? What is the convergence time per method (approximate)?
    \item What are the strengths and weaknesses of different methods of attitude/orbit determination?
    \item \textit{Addition post reasearch:} Why are so many people still using EKFs for orbit determination? What kind of errors are they maintaining?
    \item \textit{Addition post reasearch:} How are people handling the "tennis-racket" phenomemon (i.e., unbalanced rotation about an axis which does not run through the center of mass? Do these models take that into account?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{AI Search Compared With Databases}
I didn't end up having enough time to retreive every paper that the AI searched, as it ended up coming back with a list of 57 papers given the prompt above, specifically asking to exclude the papers I already had in the collection. I have tried this exercise in the past, and one of the things I did differently this time was to setup a model context protocol (MCP) which allows papers to be searched via the internet. Now, whenever I ask for literature review things, the AI will query up to real databases based on my search tool (called \href{https://github.com/openags/paper-search-mcp}{Paper Search MCP}). I think one of the things it's bad at it coming up with conclusions from the output of the papers. For example, I might read something new in a paper, then go look for other papers with that subject. Since AI doesn't have access to the PDF most of the time, it's just looking at the abstract for results. Sometimes, careful study of the paper is important to understand where the next search direction is or what questions you should ask. That being said, as far as I can tell every DOI I searched again was real, but I didn't check all 57 papers. It was much better at searching for what I was asking rather than keywords, as it found several papers that I can tell are very useful like Morai et. al.(2025) that definitely wouldn't have been caught by a keyword search.
